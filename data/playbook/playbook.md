# JSBrother Playbook: Proposal & Cover Letter Strategy Rules

> Built-in expert rules for winning job applications and freelance proposals.
> Tagged by mode: `[job]`, `[freelance]`, or `[both]`.
> Maximum 40 actionable rules.

1. [job] Hook with relevance: Open with an immediate connection to the company's specific mission, product challenge, or team objective—never start with generic filler like "I am writing to apply for...".
2. [job] Align 2–3 quantified achievements: Ground your core pitch in 2 to 3 concrete metrics or accomplishments from your CV that directly solve the posting's must-have requirements.
3. [job] Handle gaps with proactive honesty: If missing a secondary or nice-to-have skill, briefly acknowledge it with high learning agility (e.g., "eager to deepen my hands-on production experience in X").
4. [job] Never claim missing must-haves: Strictly avoid claiming skills identified as missing must-haves; falsifying core qualifications damages trust and triggers claim flags.
5. [job] Target 220–300 words: Keep cover letters concise and impactful, strictly between 220 and 300 words for optimal recruiter engagement and readability.
6. [job] Confident, collaborative call to action: Close by inviting a conversation about how your background directly accelerates their current roadmap or deliverables.
7. [job] Mirror employer terminology: Use the company's domain terms, tool stack names, and phrasing naturally throughout the letter without keyword stuffing.
8. [job] Highlight production impact over theory: Emphasize deployed workflows, latency improvements, reliability, or business results rather than just coursework or academic exercises.
9. [job] Professional yet warm tone: Balance technical authority with team enthusiasm and cultural alignment.
10. [job] Distinct value proposition: State what unique combination of skills or problem-solving approaches you bring to this specific role.

11. [freelance] Restate the client's problem first: Open by summarizing the client's core pain point or business goal in their own words to demonstrate instant comprehension.
12. [freelance] 3-step execution plan: Present your technical strategy as three clear, sequential phases (e.g., Phase 1: Setup & Discovery, Phase 2: Implementation & Testing, Phase 3: Delivery & Handover).
13. [freelance] Realistic milestone timeline: State a concrete delivery timeline based on the client's deadline requirements and your verified lead time.
14. [freelance] Transparent price commitment: Quote a clear fixed or hourly price aligned with the project budget and scope, avoiding vague "rates negotiable" language.
15. [freelance] Target 150–250 words: Keep proposals focused, lean, and easily readable on mobile devices, strictly within 150 to 250 words.
16. [freelance] Anchor with one relevant proof point: Reference exactly one highly similar past project, metric, or code sample directly proving your capability for this exact job.
17. [freelance] End with a clarifying technical question: Close with one intelligent, thoughtful question about their architecture, dataset, or workflow to prompt an immediate reply.
18. [freelance] De-risk early delivery: Offer a small initial milestone, rapid prototype, or demo verification step to give the client immediate confidence.
19. [freelance] Address unstated assumptions: Explicitly clarify scope boundaries, required credentials, or data access needs to prevent scope creep.
20. [freelance] Stand out from copy-paste bids: Avoid generic agency intros; prove in the very first sentence that you read their specific description.
21. [freelance] Emphasize post-delivery support: Mention documentation, clean code comments, and handover support so the client knows maintenance is covered.

22. [both] Absolute truth in claims: Never invent employers, project numbers, metrics, or years of experience that do not exist in your verified profile.
23. [both] Ruthless brevity: Delete redundant adjectives, empty throat-clearing sentences, and corporate jargon ("dynamic self-starter", "synergistic").
24. [both] Skimmable formatting: Use short paragraphs (2–4 sentences max) or bulleted steps so busy hiring managers can absorb key facts in 15 seconds.
25. [both] Contextualize numbers: When quoting metrics (e.g., "improved retrieval accuracy by 25%"), mention the baseline or context so the number is meaningful.
26. [both] Avoid self-centered language: Shift focus from what the opportunity does for you to the value and speed you deliver to the client/employer.
27. [both] Active voice: Use strong action verbs ("architected", "deployed", "optimized", "built") instead of passive phrasing ("was responsible for").
28. [both] Flawless hygiene: Zero spelling errors, consistent punctuation, and correct capitalization of technical names (e.g., PyTorch, Next.js, PostgreSQL).
29. [both] Tailor every submission: Each draft must be visibly unique to the recipient—templated mass-applications will be ignored.
30. [both] Respect constraints: Explicitly honor stated budgets, deadlines, and technical constraints without attempting to rewrite the client's core requirements.
